Elon Musk: “In 36 Months, the Cheapest Place to Put AI Will Be Space”

Terrestrial utilities take seven years to interconnect a single gigawatt. In orbit, solar flux is 1,361 W/m² 24/7 with zero clouds, no real estate permits, and a 3-Kelvin heatsink. Here is why Elon Musk believes frontier training clusters will leave Earth by 2029.

When frontier AI labs talk about scaling laws today, they rarely talk about algorithms first. They talk about substations, turbines, and transmission lines. In a nearly three-hour deep dive on the Dwarkesh Podcast, Elon Musk argued that terrestrial infrastructure constraints have become so severe that orbital compute will undercut Earth-based data centers much sooner than anyone anticipates.

We analyzed the full conversation across orbital thermodynamics, Starship payload curves, and humanoid manufacturing to extract the core thesis.

Insight 1 The grid interconnect queue is longer than the entire lifespan of modern AI

On Earth, the bottleneck is not capital or chips — it is power delivery. Building a multi-gigawatt facility requires navigating local utility commissions, ordering high-voltage transformers with 4-year lead times, and negotiating rights-of-way that take upwards of seven years.

"If you want to spin up a 5-gigawatt cluster on the ground in the US, you are basically fighting every regulatory agency, every environmental review, and a queue of utility interconnects that doesn't clear until the 2030s. The technology changes completely every 18 months, but the utility moves on a 40-year depreciation schedule."

— Elon Musk, Feb 2026

Because frontier models depreciate rapidly, an infrastructure deployment cycle that takes half a decade is functionally terminal for competitive labs.

Insight 2 Solar flux in Low Earth Orbit is continuous, unfiltered, and free

Outside the atmosphere, solar radiation yields roughly 1,361 watts per square meter — nearly double what ground-based panels receive on a clear summer noon, and infinitely more than they receive at night or under cloud cover. In sun-synchronous dusk-dawn orbits, an array is perpetually illuminated.

When you combine continuous solar energy with ultra-thin gallium arsenide or perovskite photovoltaic sheets deployed in zero-gravity without heavy structural mounts, the power capture per kilogram of launched mass outclasses terrestrial solar farms.

Insight 3 Heat dissipation in a vacuum: Radiators to the 3-Kelvin cosmic background

The standard critique of orbital data centers is thermal management: in a vacuum, you have no air convection and no water cooling. Elon explained that radiative cooling follows the Stefan-Boltzmann law ($T^4$), and deep space provides an ambient temperature of approximately 2.7 Kelvin.

By deploying thin, deployable liquid-loop heat pipe radiators running at 70°C (343 K), clusters can radiate hundreds of megawatts of thermal energy directly into the cosmic void. The surface area required is large, but thin polymer heat-spreaders weigh relatively little when rolled up inside a Starship fairing.

Insight 4 Starship payload economics cross the cost parity threshold under $100/kg

The entire economic equation hinges on launch cost. Musk stated that as Starship achieves full reuse and high flight cadence, the cost to Low Earth Orbit drops under $100 per kilogram, eventually targeting $20-$30/kg. At those rates, launching an AI satellite with custom radiation-hardened ASIC blades and lightweight PV panels costs less in upfront Capex than constructing an equivalent ground facility with land acquisition, water rights, and backup diesel generation.

"Within 36 months, launch economics and terrestrial power delays cross lines on a chart. After that crossover, building a gigawatt cluster on Earth will seem as backward as running fiber cables across the ocean when you have laser links."

— Elon Musk
Insight 5 Optimus humanoids will manufacture the TeraFab supply chain

Musk also connected the compute race to Tesla's humanoid robot program, Optimus. High-volume manufacturing of space-bound compute modules cannot rely on traditional factory labor due to global demographic declines and extreme precision requirements. Tesla is gearing up internal production lines where Optimus units handle automated component assembly, wafer packaging inspection, and cleanroom handling 24 hours a day.

The bottom line

Musk's timeline of 36 months may be characteristically aggressive, but the fundamental physics of terrestrial power scarcity versus orbital solar abundance are undeniable. As Starship enters operational cadence, space is transitioning from a science expedition into the premier compute backplane of intelligence.
