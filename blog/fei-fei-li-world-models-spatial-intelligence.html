After LLMs: Fei-Fei Li on Spatial Intelligence, World Models, and World Labs

Language models can write essays and code, but they cannot navigate a cluttered room or predict where a dropped glass will shatter. AI pioneer Fei-Fei Li explains why the next frontier of artificial intelligence is spatial intelligence — grounding models in 3D physics and geometry.

Source Episode
After LLMs: Spatial Intelligence and World Models — Fei-Fei Li & Justin Johnson, World Labs  ·  Nov 25, 2025  ·  1h 0m
Full transcript →

Fifteen years ago, Dr. Fei-Fei Li catalyzed the modern deep learning revolution by creating ImageNet. Today, having witnessed the meteoric rise of Transformer-based language models, she argues that text-only architectures have encountered a fundamental cognitive wall.

On the Latent Space podcast with Justin Johnson (cofounder of World Labs), Dr. Li laid out the blueprint for Spatial Intelligence and World Labs' generative world model platform, Marble.

Insight 1 Evolution spent 540 million years on vision before language ever emerged

The Cambrian explosion occurred when primordial organisms evolved vision — the ability to perceive spatial geometry, distinguish predator from prey, and navigate 3D environments. Language evolved mere tens of thousands of years ago as a compressed abstraction on top of that perceptual bedrock.

"Nature spent half a billion years perfecting spatial intelligence. Vision is not just passive sensor input; it is active exploratory physics. LLMs have ingested all of human text, but they possess no intuitive understanding of volume, occlusions, friction, or gravity."

— Fei-Fei Li, Nov 2025
Insight 2 Generative video is not a world model; 3D geometry is

Justin Johnson explained why 2D pixel diffusion models (like Sora) fall short of true world models. When a video diffusion model pans a camera around an object, the object's backside frequently warps, changes texture, or hallucinates new geometry because the model has no persistent 3D state.

World Labs' architecture, embodied in Marble, generates fully realized 3D persistent environments with consistent lighting, spatial coordinates, and physical collision boundaries.

Insight 3 The bridge to humanoid robotics and embodied autonomy

Roboticists currently struggle with synthetic data generation: training a robot arm in simulation requires painstaking hand-crafted CAD assets and game engines. Spatial world models allow agents to train inside billions of photorealistic, physically reactive generated worlds at scale, solving the sample-efficiency crisis in robotics.

The bottom line

The first phase of the AI boom mastered words and symbols. The next phase will master space, matter, and movement. Through spatial intelligence, machines will finally understand the physical universe we inhabit.
